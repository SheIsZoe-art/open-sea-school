THE SEA UNIVERSITY — AGE-FIRST EDITION · DEEPER DIFFICULTY REVISION
Captain Claw’s offline school · 31 October 2026 · English

OPEN THE SCHOOL

Open OPEN-SEA-SCHOOL.html in a modern web browser. The single file contains
lessons, illustrations, games, generated sounds and local progress.
No account, installation, subscription or connection is needed.
On a phone, choose a browser instead of a static file preview.

810 PLAYABLE COURSES

9 approximate age bands, from 6–7 to 14–15.
9 subjects, with 10 courses per subject and band: 90 courses at each age.
Subjects: mathematics; English and literacy; French as a world language;
science; history and geography; life and critical thinking; visual arts;
music; computing and technology.

Age is a flexible starting point, not a rule about what a person should know.
US Grade 1–9 hints are approximate. Entry ages, state standards, schools and
individual pathways differ. Common Core maths/literacy and NGSS topic references
inform selected content; this is not a certified or complete US curriculum.
Some humanities examples remain international or French.

The catalogue uses 25 assessed interaction families and course-specific content
and configurations. Courses share controls and engines: this is not a claim of
810 unrelated software engines or 810 complete textbook chapters.

MORE THAN SHUFFLING THE ANSWERS

New expedition varies supported quantities, images, patterns, destinations and
formulations. Reset keeps the same setup. Factual content banks remain finite;
reordering a historical statement does not create a new historical fact.

Distribution tasks now include unequal orders, leftovers, missing amounts,
partly filled containers and relational instructions. Equal-share courses keep
their mathematical meaning, but can begin with unequal loads needing repair.
Older age bands introduce ratios, implicit unknowns, unit rates, linear models
and constrained supply relationships where appropriate.

The captain’s optional Clawful tool moves a chosen group of up to ten objects
between containers. It does not solve the manifest: the learner chooses quantity,
source and destination and still has to check totals and relationships.

Other tasks include scaled bar charts, reading evidence, editing, typed or
tile-built words, controlled comparisons, allocation problems with multiple
valid answers, rotations, musical structures and program traces.

CAPTAIN CLAW

The visible lobster’s question mark opens three levels of help: the goal,
a method, and an explanation. Help has no penalty. Escape closes the panel.
The captain also has 80 positive jokes about the sea, nature, lobsters, learning,
repair and ecology. The joke selection adapts to the age band.

Captain’s cove includes a joke chest, a free silly studio for changing the
captain’s hat and conducting a gentle dance, and discoverable game corners.

DISCOVERY AND RELOCKING

Two cove corners are free immediately. Further corners appear after 3, 6, 10,
15, 22, 30 and 40 distinct completed courses. Some later entries cost two or
three practice tickets.

A successful fresh course expedition earns one ticket, with at most 30 saved.
Resetting and rechecking the same expedition does not earn another ticket.
Old progress can provide a small one-time carryover when the wallet is first
created. Tickets are used on entry to an optional bonus challenge. The same
challenge can be retried without paying again; a new entry can require tickets.

Some bonus corners therefore relock when tickets are insufficient. There are
no payments, calendar deadlines, inactivity penalties or lost streaks.
Lessons, the separate puzzle reef and earned work remain accessible.
Bonus corners are themed versions of puzzle families, not exclusive educational
content that must be earned before learning.

BRAIN-TEASER REEF

Nine age-adjusted puzzle families practise specific ways of thinking:
- Matching-memory cards.
- Lighthouse tower planning with legal disc moves.
- Generated unique 4×4, 6×6 or 9×9 number squares.
- Reversible lagoon lamps.
- Two- or three-stream sequences with steps, direction changes and distractors.
- Single-feature rules progressing to Boolean and conditional multi-round rules.
- Rotations, reflections and compound colour transformations.
- Generated maze routes, later with mandatory stamps and detours.
- Combining signed values, item count, space and budget constraints.

Puzzles have no timer or IQ score. They are task-specific practice, not a
diagnosis, a cognitive assessment or a promise of general cognitive improvement.
The separate reef is always open without tickets.

OTHER SPACES

Life & Better World has 24 practical project cards about cooperation, privacy,
repair, reuse, access, information checking and community projects. Each links
to related skill practice. Check what actually helps rather than inventing
impact claims or judging people’s circumstances.

The creative sandpit lets you rake, dig, place shells, build castles and undo.
There is no scored answer. Sand marks are temporary, not journal entries.
The original 16-game arcade and mixed six-subject voyages also remain available.

KEEP YOUR WORK

Export the JSON journal regularly. Local-file storage can be cleared or separated
when a file moves. Imports replace the current journal after confirmation.
Older English results and drafts remain readable. Course medals, practice records,
arcade medals and cove tickets are retained separately.
English and French editions use separate storage. One journal is active at a
time on a shared device. Drafts are exported, so avoid private information.

MOUSE, TOUCH, KEYBOARD AND SOUND

Moving games allow dragging or selecting a piece and activating a destination
heading with tap or Tab and Enter. Matching uses a left label and right partner.
Word tasks allow typing or letter tiles. Bar charts use plus/minus controls.
Music is generated locally only after a music control is pressed; visual scores
also work without audio. No microphone is used. Stop cancels pending notes.

WHAT THIS STILL DOES NOT REPLACE

Several years of school include sustained reading, writing, speech, movement,
social interaction, rich investigations, feedback and independent assessment.
This catalogue does not supply all of those. It has not received an exhaustive
state-standard audit or independent teacher validation, and awards no recognised
qualification. Further content, teacher review, learner trials and real-device
accessibility testing are needed.

The four original concept files and the French edition have not been modified.
No rights to original materials are reassigned by this package.

Knowledge is not a luxury. It belongs to everyone.

DIFFICULTY REVISION

Age now changes task demands, not only labels or board size. Memory moves from
identical pictures to words, arithmetic and equivalent fractions. Towers can start
scrambled and restrict transfer links. Number grids are uniquely generated with
varied sizes and clues. Lamps can use different toggle stencils. Older rule tasks
change features between successful rounds. Maze layouts are generated afresh.

Arcade tasks now vary manifests, multi-step changes, variables on both sides,
several ingredient controls, multi-output circuit rules, BCE/CE records and
cost-plus-load packing. Older course programs must navigate generated obstacles;
rhythms can require an unseen transformed bar; melodies use relative key changes;
compositions coordinate relationships; some binary tasks start from hexadecimal.

Age is still only a guide. These designed task demands are not measured claims
about the difficulty any particular learner will experience. Choose another age
freely if a task is too easy or too demanding. Factual content banks remain finite.
